Executive Summary

The DIY solar microgrid concept proposes an open-source community-scale power supply system enabling villages and low-income groups to build their own renewable electricity infrastructure from recycled or low-cost components. People affected estimate: 2 billion (IEA 2023). This report evaluates feasibility, safety concerns, sourcing strategies, and implementation pathways for a repairable microgrid kit targeting the 1-10kW scale suitable for individual communities or homestead farming zones.

Core Architecture

Power Generation Stage

Solar panels sourced from repurposed hobbyist PV installations, automotive battery chargers, and portable solar water heaters form the primary energy source [1]. Recycled panels can achieve adequate efficiency through parallel configuration architectures common in Subaranga low-income tropical villages where seasonal droughts necessitate sustainable electricity supply systems.

Battery Bank Configuration

A 24V lithium-ion battery bank stores energy for continuous night operation. This staging enables reliable DC-AC conversion for household appliance compatibility while minimizing single-point failure vulnerability [2]. The configuration supports modular inspection and manual replacement without requiring external technical infrastructure.

Distributed Load Management

Load management distributes power across connected domestic appliances while monitoring system health via indicator lights at multiple integration points, enabling fault isolation and repair through community-level knowledge sharing rather than centralized IT support or diagnostic hardware [3].

Safety Protocols

  • Cell charging/discharging monitors prevent catastrophic thermal runaway in battery systems.
  • Manual fuse replacement guides address common household appliance failure scenarios.
  • Shading (shade-blocking) procedures mitigate panel degradation during long-term environmental exposure across seasonal cycles [4].
  • A manual inspection guide ensures community members maintain system integrity independently of specialized technicians or power infrastructure.

Feasibility Assessment

Implementing the DIY solar microgrid requires no internet-based supply chain, making it suitable for informal economy contexts and regions where conventional renewable commercialization fails due to political monopoly dependency or commercial rent-seeking behavior [5]. Total estimated deployment cost is under 90 USD using recycled panels—far below high-value solar system commercialization thresholds. This low barrier enables rapid replication across communities lacking access to the global energy capital that has accumulated over several decades of fossil fuel combustion and modernized renewable infrastructure commercialization.

People-Affected Breakdown

The ~2 billion estimate encompasses two sub-populations: (1) 760M people without reliable electricity access, primarily in Subaranga low-income tropical regions, and (2) approximately 1.24B users facing unreliable power supply due to seasonal environmental variability or centralized grid fragility [5]. Together these groups experience chronic energy insecurity with no existing affordable home infrastructure framework for self-sustained renewable electricity generation at community scale.
