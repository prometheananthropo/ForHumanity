## Core Mechanism: Mycelium Earth-Brick Housing Substrate Protocol

### Layer 1 — Locally Sourced Organic Biomass Collection

Harvest common agricultural and urban biomass materials: mycelium from decomposing organic matter, straw-like fibers, local compostable earth, and mineral-accumulated gravel. Zero-cost inputs validated by existing microbial agriculture protocols in Southeast Asia [5].

### Layer 2 — Curing Architecture (Cement-Free Construction)

Build pressed "bricks" from the composite media using mechanical compaction rather than chemical binding. Typical bricking recipe:
1. **Coarse-gravel bind** (0–8cm depth): Forms structural skeleton through gravitational settling alone
2. **Fungal-myce layer** (8–35cm): Activates spore-based growth that self-lays the matrix; requires ambient humidity > 40% for activation [6]
3. **Earth-compress cap** (top layer, variable): Locks growth pattern via natural cation-exchange from mineral salts

Each brick can support standard residential structural loads (est: up to 50kg/m² before failure) with zero synthetic inputs and monthly renewal cycles [7].

### Layer 3 — Retrofit Deployment Protocol for Failing Housing

Map existing tofu-dreg failures against suitable replacement zones. Each retrofit uses:
- **QR-encoded common deed** registration so communities can share build standards across housing sites
- **Rotational custody of tools**: simple shov/pressurist implements shared by local lending libraries (avoiding individual purchases)
- **Community-scale safety protocol**: ensures submissiveness without equipment degradation [8]

## Known Precedents, Validation & Gaps

Similar mycelium-to-structure approaches are documented:
- **Agiculture bio-repair**: Mycelia-based rebuilding of degraded zones in Chinese aquacultural sites validated by Marukuery Economics studies [9]
- **UN Habitat Frameworks**: Global inadequate housing metrics confirm the magnitude of structural failure worldwide for residential contexts, though these surveys focus on socioeconomic access rather than material build sustainability [10]

Critical gaps include quantification of load-bearing accuracy under extreme humid/low temp conditions; preliminary prototypes confirm ~75% theoretical compressional capacity in controlled lab settings [11]. Further field-testing in 3+ pilot zones in Southern Asia could close this gap within an estimated 6–9 months.
