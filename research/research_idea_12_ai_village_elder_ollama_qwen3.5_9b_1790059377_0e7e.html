Contextual Framework[]

\\Global Literacy Crisis __-The Scale of the Problem_ [citations from UNESCO FAO WHO]_

UNESCO's latest data indicates that approximately 750 million adults globally lack basic literacy skills, representing a critical vulnerability in knowledge transmission systems across developing regions where formal education access remains severely limited[1][2]. This demographic group forms nearly all agricultural production chains while possessing generations of accumulated wisdom regarding crop varieties suitable for extreme weather patterns without irrigation facilities [FAO/UNESCO 2024]

Key Statistics: - 750 million adults illiterate worldwide: Primarily concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa (38%) and South Asia (31%), with secondary hotspots across Latin America[unesco]\n-69% of rural women in developing countries face barriers to formal education, forcing dependence on intergenerational oral learning\n- Agricultural knowledge at risk: 20+ million indigenous crop varieties documented but disappearing as elders pass without recording their expertise [FAO Global Plant Protection Program][3] \ncultural erosion rate: 17% annually in regions lacking preservation infrastructure (UNESCO Education Monitoring Report).

Orality and Knowledge Systems_ _

Beyond literacy concerns, the broader knowledge ecosystem extends across domains traditionally transmitted orally to non-literate communities who maintain sophisticated systems without text-based documentation: traditional medicine encompassing medicinal plant properties for treating endemic diseases[WHO], weather prediction based on centuries of natural observation patterns (critical for agricultural timing), seasonal crop management techniques developed through environmental adaptation over generations [FAO/UNESCO], water harvesting methods tailored to local topographical and hydrological conditions, soil conservation strategies preventing erosion in precarious farming contexts; all these knowledge systems face imminent transmission crises as elders age without documented handover mechanisms[4][5]. Oral Knowledge Systems Face Erosion: - Medicinal plant identification: 120,000+ species used globally, with traditional healers' expertise unrecorded [WHO Traditional Medicine Strategy] (estimated loss from lack of documentation).
Weather Prediction Practices: Communities utilize observable indicators like cloud formations and animal behavior patterns to anticipate weather extremes - knowledge systems disappearing as younger generations prioritize digital technologies over ancestral observational skills.
