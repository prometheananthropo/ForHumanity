Solar Microgrid DIY Kit: Village-Scale Replicable Energy Access

Executive Summary

This research report analyzes the technical, social and economic dimensions of village-scale solar microgrids built from refurbished or used photovoltaic modules. The target capacity range is 1kW–10 kW community systems that can be assembled locally with minimal specialized training and complete repairability for long-term operation in developing regions where grid access remains unreliable or non-existent. An estimated 2 billion people experience electricity insecurity globally, including 760 million without any connection at all [1].

People Affected Estimate Verification

The International Energy Agency (IEA) estimates approximately 760 million people lack basic electricity while an additional roughly 2 billion face unreliable service—both categories directly benefit from DIY microgrid deployments that require no infrastructure permits or expensive interconnection fees: ~2,8B potential beneficiaries across emerging economies in Africa and Asia [1]. This estimate is supported by data showing approximately 60% of Sub-Saharan countries still depend heavily on fossil fuels with unreliable generation patterns while intermittent PV can provide baseline energy continuously when paired with storage.
