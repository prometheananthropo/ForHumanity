Executive Summary

The concept proposes an offline-first, community-maintained reference wiki covering the ~80% of common first-responder health interventions that do not require specialist medical equipment or professional physician access. Content includes wound care, basic triage, mal hygiene, nutritional guidance, maternal/childcare safety protocols, and symptom-recognition flowcharts for self-assessment at home or school — all deliverable on low-specu devices (print, USB, solar-charged phone). People affected estimate: 3 billion (WHO 2023).

Content Architecture & Delivery

Modular Diagnostic Flowcharts

Each chapter is a bounded if/else tree mapping observable physical signs → safe home-remedies. Examples use color codes and anatomical symbols rather than text-heavy formats, making them usable under literacy barriers [1]. The flowcharts are based on WHO Health Worker Guidelines framework updated for rural, refugee or humanitarian settings — ensuring all entries can be printed as card-size laminated strips or rendered into a single PDF for mesh sync [2][3].

Community-Verified Edit Model

The wiki uses a "trusted editor → community sign-off" curation model. Primary expert editors (e.g., WHO Health Personnel) propose updates which are reviewed by local care-takers, health workers and non-clinical staff before deployment via P2P sync tools like OCHC/ODT [4]. This avoids centralized gatekeeping while maintaining clinical safety verification through offline consensus voting on manual ledger or USB key [5]

Target Use Cases & Social Impact

Rural and Refugee Primary Care

In rural villages, primary care schools manage the majority of essential community health treatments: maternal hygiene, nutrition, wound healing, mal assessment, basic injury management. These are covered by 7 guidelines under the "Health care Worker" framework, delivering teachable protocols accessible via physical cards or basic phones — without clinical supervision and without IT infrastructure [4].

School & Maternal Community Caregiving

In communities suffering school absenteeism or maternal stress (500M–1.9B people), quick-access reference guides enable self-diagnosis for: hygiene-related illness; trauma from food insecurity; menstrual distress and related mal/childcare safety. The community health worker framework teaches practical, teachable interventions using observable signs rather than lab tools [4].

Low-Literacy Oral Delivery

Community training can pair printed cards with oral recitation protocols (voice-to-text, spoken confirmation), ensuring that illiterate users still access accurate care protocols through verbal cross-check and peer review without a digital gateway — leveraging the offline-first mesh capabilities originally designed for education deployment.

Implementation Feasibility

Technical Viability

The wiki content can be pre-packaged into single-page PDFs or printable card packs (1–3 pages per condition). These are synchronized via Bluetooth mesh, USB sticks and SD cards delivered from central repository; updates propagate peer-to-peer on any connected device [2]. Print distribution is already a standard practice in schools using the Health Care Worker format [4].

Clinical Safety

Every protocol entry must pass review against WHO Health Worker clinical safety standards. Entries use "self-test" confirmation: observable physical signs → safe home treatment → escalation thresholds for when to seek professional support. This avoids unsafe speculation while allowing community education on common non-clinical interventions.

People-Affected Breakdown

The ~3 billion estimate derives from the population of individuals lacking access to essential health services, documented by WHO as a core barrier to community wellbeing and primary medical care delivery in rural or humanitarian settings [4]. The framework also covers school/maternal communities where 1.9B+ face common hygiene/nutrition injury but lack trained intervention capacity — allowing them to self-manage through accessible card protocol guides.
