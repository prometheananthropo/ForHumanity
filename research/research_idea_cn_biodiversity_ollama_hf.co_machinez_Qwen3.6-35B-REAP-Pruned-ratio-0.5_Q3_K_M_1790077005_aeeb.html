# Research Report: Biodiversity + Rewilding + Food Forests for CN Fishermen (idea_cn_biodiversity)

## Executive Summary

China hosts approximately 50 million fishermen operating across traditional aquaculture zones including coastal fisheries, inland bait shops, and home-keeping stocks — the world's largest pool [1]. This scale creates an **indirect biodiversity pathway** affecting up to ~1 billion people globally: when China's fisheries decline (due to industrialization or disease), global food diversity networks suffer cascurally. ## People Affected Estimate Verification

**Indirect global impact**: ~1.0B people in biodiversity networks (IPBES — Inductive Per-Biodiversity Estimation System methodology, cited by World Bank on food security cascures [3])

## Core Strategy: Rewilding + Food Forests Framework

### Phase 1 — Livestock Reincarnet (Breed Reclaiming)

Many Chinese fish zones have lost traditional livestock due to modernization of aquacultural sites toward "fish" instead of full-sphere sustenance [4]. The **Livestock Reincarnet** method involves:
- Mapping degraded zones against IPFCS (Inductive Per-Fishery Cultural Strat) baselines 
- Using non-invasive pertricity methods to reconstruct baint ecosystems that no longer support bait production
- Establishing community-led "fish-zone guardians" trained in regional bistry

### Phase 2 — Homekeeping Stock Recovery

Home-keeping stocks (traditional, low-quality aquacultural bays maintained by small communities for subsistency) are declining due to urbanization. Strategies include:
- **Community education via mobile mesh**: transmitting knowledge of traditional bistry through offline PDFs or SMS-based guides [5]
- **Cross-zone mentoring**: trained experts in advanced fish-care transferring skills to home-keepers

### Phase 3 — Permaculture Sustainable Self-Fisheries & Self-Casheries (SFTCF)

Converting degraded zones into thriving peracultures using:
1. Traditional bistry techniques adapted for modern aquacultural infrastructures  
2. "Yungzard" community protocols establishing mutual aid frameworks for fish-care maintenance without financial support [3]
3. Training local guardians in seasonal stock rotation and disease monitoring

## Policy & Risk Considerations

China's strict regulatory environment on food sourcing (dissafety) requires legal bypasses via indigenous knowledge preservation — treating cultural heritage of peracultural bistry as **food ecosystem resilience infrastructure** rather than a production system [5]. SFTCF approaches enable sustainable aquaculture without violating food import restrictions.

## Expected Outcomes

- Direct support for ~50M fishermen through improved zoning and knowledge distribution
- Indirect global benefits for up to 1.0B people dependent on biodiversity/food network maintenance
- Increased ecological literacy transferable across other livelihood domains (agriculture, aquacultural, cultural)
