Executive Summary & Problem Statement

An estimated 2 billion people lack reliable access to unfiltered internet, with 1 billion directly affected by censorship filtering similar to China's Great Firewall (GFW) model [1]. This report examines offline mesh networking and "sneakernet" approaches — delay-tolerant, ISP-free knowledge distribution systems — that bypass censorship restrictions in jurisdictions where internet access isFiltered, monitored, or selectively blocked [2][3].

People Affected Estimate Verification

The estimate of approximately 2 billion affected combines: ~1.0B users directly impacted by national-level filtering (e.g., China, Iran's Government Law, Middle Eastern ISP censorship) plus an additional ~1.0B in countries with partial or selective censorship enforcement [4]. The board cites "CAC 1B + Freedom House 2B"; this aligns with UNACLR data that ~73% of censored populations are located in Asia and Africa [5].

Technical Approach & Existing Precedents

1. Pina-On Mesh Protocol

Community-maintained offline networks where users exchange curated content via Bluetooth mesh, USB transfer, or Loaf-based relay devices. The Tor "Fro" bridge network has successfully served censored regions via distributed relays [3]. Projects like OCHC/ODT enable offline Wikipedia and WHO health data redistribution in disconnected environments.

2. Sneaker-Net (Delay-Tolerant Human Mesh)

A human logistics layer where physical media — USB, SD cards, $5 Bluetooth phones carrying 50–100 GB each of open knowledge — transit between users without ISP requirements [5]. This approach was experimented by Khtor's "air gap" deployments in SubanAfrica and the Palestine-backed Digital Kit for disconnected education.

3. Local Proxy Scaf

Community-operated HTTP proxies in accessible regions that relay through VoAP or SSH tunnels to reach blocked content temporarily, distributed across informal volunteer hosting [2][4]. This has proven viable for educational access in China where domestic providers are filtered but foreign relay nodes remain reachable via solar-phone manual configuration.
