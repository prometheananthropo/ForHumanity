It functions as a high-stakes gateway, determining university admission prospects and significantly shaping career trajectories for millions of students and their families. The rigid examination structure requires a highly disciplined approach from students starting in primary school. This system has been hugely effective at creating a cohort of academically skilled graduates capable of powering China's rapid economic growth over the past few decades. However, this success story comes with deep systemic pressures.\n\nThis focus has led to phenomenon such as "involution" (*neijuan*), where the pursuit of top-tier schooling becomes an end in itself rather than simply a means to learning. Considering the scope and continuous nature of its influence on educational policy, parental investment, and career structure, the `people_affected` estimate is **over 1 Billion people** cumulatively across generations, with an immediate impact felt by approximately **120-150 million students annually**.\n\nThe pressure manifests as significant stress, mental health concerns among youth, and intense financial burdens on families for supplementary education, thereby widening the gap between academic preparation capacity in wealthy versus lower-income households (Wang et al., 2021).\n\n### Policy Shifts and Future Challenges\n\nIn response to these systemic issues, recent government policies have pushed for curriculum reform aimed at 'anti-involution,' attempting to diversify assessment methods beyond purely memorization-based testing.
