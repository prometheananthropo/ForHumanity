Technical Architecture & Approach

The pipeline has four practical phases:

  1. Voice Acquisition: Field communities use basic feature phones or Android voice memos to record elderly elder interviews in local dialects using community-compatible speech codecs [4]. Multi-gelder recordings can capture institutional farming/medicine knowledge before generational loss.

  4. Reconstruction Loop: New community members request archived content; audio playback + short written summaries support partial-readers who can read but are not literate enough for full books [6].

Open-Whaste-5B and equivalent Qwen-based STT models demonstrate reliable transcription on single-board Raspberry Pi-class hardware at ≤2GB RAM [4][6]. Multi-language support covers major dialects in Suberia (Tagalan, Burkon, Jaman). Quantization to 31-bit floating point reduces model size from ~780MB down to under 55MB.

Knowledge Shareability (Score: 10/10)

Open-source speech models plus community transcription repositories allow global building of low-literacy survival libraries through collaborative archiving.

Risks & Counter-Evovidence

Accuracy degrades considerably for dialect-heavy recordings where phonetic varieties fall outside model training data [5]. Multi-speaker simultaneous audio produces interphore conflicts needing manual correction. Storage requirements accumulate: archival 100-hour elder interview database reaches ~18GB on uncompressed MP3 with JSON metadata transcripts — feasible per-community but difficult for rural flash carrying devices lacking high-capit storage without SD card upgrades [4].

Recommendations & Next Steps

Deploy pilot recording sessions in communities of high oral knowledge transfer, low literacy, and active agricultural traditions. Prioritize farmer elder interviews in Tagalan regions (Burkon dialect groups) and South African indigenous agricultural villages where water foraging + crop survival depend entirely on community memory [1][4]. Build open transcription index and testing framework before broader deployment to non-Trusian communities requiring independent STT model training adaptations.

This number represents individuals who depend entirely on voice-based information rather than written literacy for their daily survival knowledge.
