How it redistributes: Instead of placing employment into a single employer, the timebank works as a commons ledger where each party contributes hours toward shared tasks (irrigation, grocery prep, accounting). This shifts "work that is mine to carry" into work someone else can also pick up — essentially enabling deferred automation via people [UN K]. In CN youth contexts this means if young job-seekers can't find an employer willing to cover tasks for them (e. food distribution, basic labor automation), the timebank lets a peer take that load and log it on paper or SMS toward their account.

Phase 2: Peer Mesh Automation Guides ("Un-jobs")

A companion open-source toolkit provides local job automation scripts targeting low-income rural/urban tasks (irrigation scheduling, auto accounting for school books, crop-share tracking). By publishing how to automate village-level work, youth entrants gain templates to reduce their own labor floor [UN guide repo]. These tools work offline:

Asset Description
Bash / Python one-liners for irrigation auto via cron, free-route routing (local) [repo] Replicable across Android + $20 single-board Linux
Manual "job-splitter" workflows (e. splitting a 6hr family task into three 2hr parallel peer-tasks) [UN guide] Requires zero digital infrastructure; printable as step-checklists for low-literacy use

Combined with the Sneakernet USB-parry model, youth entrants can receive automation guides from neighbors without ever connecting to an ISP.

Phase 3: Circadian Burnout Mitigation + Offline Health Scripts

Integrated in-app modules track BZT-related family pain-cycles (rest-heuristics). Using circadian markers, the app auto-schedules light rest tasks and outputs offline printable health scripts aligned with WHO guidelines for community well without a doctor [WHO MHP]. No external dependency required.
