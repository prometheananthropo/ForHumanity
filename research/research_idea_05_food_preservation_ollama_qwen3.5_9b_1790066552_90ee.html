Research Report: Local Food Preservation & Fermentation Knowledge Base (idea_05_food_preservation)

Executive Summary

The idea proposes a traditional fermentation approach adapted to contemporary needs, requiring no refrigeration infrastructure while simultaneously reducing food waste and improving nutritional quality. With an estimated 2.5 billion people affected according to FAO 2023 data on global food insecurity [1], this intervention targets the core mechanisms of sustainable nutrition delivery across low-resource settings worldwide.

The knowledge base synthesizes ancestral preservation techniques with accessible fermentation protocols that can be deployed without electricity, enabling communities in developing nations and underserved urban areas to protect harvests, extend shelf life naturally through microbial activity, and reduce dependence on supply chains requiring cold storage infrastructure [2][3].

1. Problem Context: Global Food Insecurity Statistics

According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) 2023 assessment, approximately one-third of all food produced for human consumption is lost or wasted globally before reaching end consumers [[1]]. This represents roughly 1.3 billion tonnes annually, equivalent to sufficient calories feeding an additional two billion people — yet these losses predominantly affect those already struggling with basic caloric sufficiency [4].

The 2.5 billion estimate of affected population encompasses food-insecure rural communities across Africa, Southeast Asia and Latin America that lack reliable refrigeration while also urban populations dependent on distributed cold-chain logistics vulnerable to infrastructure disruptions [[1]]. In contexts where commercial cooling equipment is unreliable or prohibitively expensive — an estimated $30-80 US dollars per household in informal settlements [2]
