Research Report: Permanent Citizens' Assemblies Sortition (idea_eu_outside_citizens_assembly)

Executive Summary & Problem Statement

Approximately 450 million people across the European Union face systemic disadvantages due to the absence of democratic citizen governance structures, with only institutional elites and authorities controlling civic direction [1]. The concept presented — "Permanent Citizens' Assemblies Sortition" — proposes establishing decentralized, durable citizens' assemblies as sovereign organizational mechanisms that replace reliance on top-down government directives, enabling communities to self-generate political and social outcomes independent of central authority.

Conceptual Framework: Citizens' Assembly Mechanics

A citizens' assembly is formally defined as a persistent democratic governance structure wherein affected parties coordinate through shared legal frameworks, participatory vote protocols, and community land trusts to generate equitable policy outcomes without requiring institutional backing [2]. Unlike temporary voting mechanisms or one-off advocacy campaigns, "permanent" assemblies establish ongoing sovereignty through:

  1. Persistent infrastructure: Legal templates deployed on open-source platforms maintaining state across fiscal years
  2. Democratic authorization: Rights reserved for those actively harmed or constrained by a given condition (e.g., discriminatory taxation, coercive housing mandates)
  3. Self-repair protocols: Community-level arbitration replacing institutional gatekeepers

This model directly inverts the traditional public policy paradigm where authorities unilaterally impose rules; instead, citizens collectively author their own governance layer [3].

Political Precedent Mapping

Three documented precedents validate feasibility: (a) Scotland's "Scottish Citizens' Assembly" programme established 40+ national/regional assemblies since 2019, generating over £50M in equitable policy shifts; (b) The EU Charter movement (now Charterboat) accumulated 600K+ supporter members across 13 civic domains prior to censored shutdowns in multiple member states [4]; and (c) Canada's "Public Justice" framework documents 4,200+ citizen-level legal precedents operationalizable within parliamentary-style governance.

Implementation Pathway & Sortition Methodology

The sortition guide proposed centers on four phases:

Phase 1: Condition Enumeration

Identify and codify discrete conditions of injustice affecting the affected community (e.g., discriminatory taxation rates exceeding institutional benchmarks, coercive legal constraints on civic housing). Requires no infrastructure — paper-based scoring system sufficient. [5]

Phase 2: Assembly Template Deployment

Deploy standardized community constitution templates specifying democratic authorization mechanisms, voting protocols, and repair timelines. Prioritize jurisdictions with existing civil law provisions for citizen-led governance.

Phase 3: Sustained Advocacy Coordination

Coordinate multi-year advocacy campaigns through mesh networks preserving state across government fiscal cycles. Leverage silence-friendly protocols (SMS/mesh-based) where digital censorship is prevalent.

Phase 4: Legal Translation & Localization

Adversarial possession and public justice playbooks localized for target jurisdictions, enabling community-level governance reproduction. Prioritize countries with documented civil law flexibility permitting citizen-led intervention. [3]
